The idea

One rollout, two different jobs

Inside test-time search, a diffusion rollout does two things at once. Exploration produces a candidate for the verifier to rank. Delivery produces the video a person actually watches.

Only delivery has to be faithful. Exploration merely has to preserve the order of candidates, a far weaker requirement, and the one nobody had measured.

So we cache exploration aggressively and re-generate only the winning seed at full compute. Because the sampler is deterministic in its seed, what ships is a genuine full-compute sample. Caching decides which candidate you get, never how good the delivered one is.

Evidence

Three findings, measured on VBench and VBench-2.0

1. Rankings survive caching

Generate every candidate twice, once at full compute and once cached, from identical seeds. Median per-prompt Spearman correlation between the two orderings is 0.905, with 72% top-1 agreement, replicated on the harder VBench-2.0 distribution.

2. When ranking breaks, it breaks cheaply

Corruption concentrates on prompts whose candidates were nearly tied to begin with, exactly where choosing wrong costs least. Median regret is zero: most prompts lose nothing at all.

3. The trajectory matters, not the budget

The obvious alternative is fewer denoising steps per candidate. At a matched two-fold saving, truncation keeps 72.6% of search's gain against caching's 90.1%, with about six times the regret. A truncated rollout honestly samples a different, shorter-schedule distribution, so its scores rank its own outputs rather than the full-compute videos that commit will deliver.

Any published cache works as the engine

Every caching family we tested drops in unchanged and lands on one capture-versus-speedup frontier. Fidelity is set by how much compute you reuse, not by which mechanism reuses it.

The honest edge

Keeping the cached draft instead of re-committing is cheaper still, but it dampens motion, and learned judges mildly prefer the dampened result. That is why commit is the default and why our keep-mode claims rest on optical flow and LPIPS rather than judge scores.
Quickstart

Three lines in your search loop

CachedSearch wraps generation rather than replacing your model, so adoption changes your search loop and nothing else.

pipe is any diffusers video pipeline. The verifier defaults to ImageReward averaged over eight frames, the scorer behind every number here; pass verifier= to use your own. One number is model-specific, the caching threshold, and the repository ships a calibration script that measures it for a new architecture in about two GPU-hours.

Full adoption guide →

# pip install -r requirements.txt
from cachedsearch import cached_search

result = cached_search(
    pipe, prompt,
    n=8, tau=0.10,
)   # verifier= to override
result.video   # full-compute sample
result.seed    # the winning seed

The method is not limited to these. It applies to any diffusion or flow-matching video model with a seed-deterministic sampler, which covers essentially every open text-to-video pipeline; these six are where we measured the numbers ourselves. LTX-Video is the honest boundary case, since no threshold we measured reached 85% capture, which is precisely what calibration is meant to reveal before deployment rather than after.
