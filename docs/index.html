Test-time search makes small video models punch far above their weight, but it pays full price for every candidate it throws away. We show that the candidate rankings search depends on survive aggressive training-free caching. That means you can explore every candidate cheaply and re-generate only the winner at full compute.

The idea

A diffusion rollout inside search does two different jobs. Exploration produces a candidate for the verifier to rank; delivery produces the video the user actually sees. Only delivery needs to be faithful. Exploration needs the verifier's ordering of candidates to survive, which is a far weaker requirement, and it is exactly the requirement nobody had measured.

Caching therefore changes which candidate you select, never the quality of what ships. That reduces the safety of the whole scheme to one measurable quantity: how faithfully cached scores rank candidates.

What we measured

Seed-matched twins: every candidate generated twice, once at full compute and once under caching, scored by the same verifier. Configured on a 50-prompt pilot grid, then re-measured on the full VBench and VBench-2.0 suites.

Rankings survive

Median per-prompt Spearman correlation is 0.905 with 72% top-1 agreement on VBench, and VBench-2.0 replicates it on a harder prompt distribution.

Failures are self-limiting

Ranking corrupts preferentially on prompts whose candidates were nearly tied to begin with, which is exactly where picking the wrong one costs least. Median regret is zero.

The budget is not enough, the trajectory is

The obvious alternative is to spend fewer denoising steps per candidate. At a matched two-fold saving, truncation keeps 72.6% of search's gain against caching's 90.1%, with roughly six times the regret. A truncated rollout is an honest sample of a different, shorter-schedule distribution, so its scores rank its own outputs rather than the videos that commit will deliver. Caching perturbs the same seed-matched trajectory, which is the property search actually needs.

Any published cache works as the engine

Every caching family we tested drops in unchanged, and all of them land on a single capture-versus-speedup frontier: fidelity is set by how much compute you reuse, not by which mechanism reuses it.

The honest edge

Keeping the cached draft instead of re-committing is cheaper still, but it dampens motion, and learned judges mildly prefer the dampened result. This is why commit is the default and why our keep-mode claims lean on optical flow and LPIPS rather than judge scores.

Use it on your model

CachedSearch is a wrapper around generation, not a new model, so adopting it is a change to your search loop rather than to your checkpoint.

from cachedsearch import cached_search

result = cached_search(pipe, prompt, verifier=my_scorer, n=8, tau=0.10)
result.video   # a genuine full-compute sample, at about 63% of best-of-8 cost

pipe is any diffusers video pipeline and my_scorer is whatever verifier you already trust. One number is model-specific: the caching threshold. Fidelity tracks architecture family rather than parameter count, so calibrate once per family with the script in the repository (about two GPU-hours) and reuse it across sizes.

The method is not limited to this list. It applies to any diffusion or flow-matching video model whose sampler is deterministic in the seed, which covers essentially every current open text-to-video pipeline. These six are simply where we measured the numbers ourselves. LTX-Video is the honest boundary case: no threshold we measured reached 85% capture, which is exactly what calibration is meant to reveal before deployment rather than after.
